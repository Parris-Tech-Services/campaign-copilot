## Final table-ready version

> Marcus, before we move the scene inside, I want to lock in Alphie's stable routine. He's been on the road long enough to know that a lame horse is a dead business, so he's being meticulous with Sace, Marrow, and Vesper. Here is exactly what he's doing to settle the team:

### 1. The Wagon & Cargo Audit

- **The Inspection:** Alphie checks the wheels, spokes, lynchpins, and hubs. He specifically inspects the **singletrees, doubletree/evener, pole, traces, and brake**. He rocks each wheel and axle gently to feel for play, wiping away the Dessarin road dust.
- **Maintenance:** He asks the stablehand for proper wagon grease — specifically **tallow or heavy axle grease** — to keep the hubs from seizing after the long haul.
- **The Load:** The two 20L casks stay low in the wagon. At roughly a **hundredweight** including the wood and liquid, they are wedged and lashed so they can't shift. He only takes the **six 750ml sample bottles** inside for Miros.

### 2. Hydration & Recovery

- **Walking the Line:** He doesn't let them stand hot in the harness. He unhooks them and walks them until their **breathing settles, the hard blowing eases**, and they are no longer standing hot.
- **Water Management:** He offers **clean water immediately**, letting them drink in watched, steady pauses while they cool, rather than leaving a winded horse unmanaged at the trough. He ensures they have constant access to plain water once they are calm.
- **Salt & Electrolytes:** He ensures they have access to **salt**; if they've sweated particularly hard, he asks whether proper electrolytes are available to be mixed into feed or offered separately — **never replacing plain water**.

### 3. Physical Audit & Animal Husbandry

- **The Harness Check:** Once the gear is off, he checks the shoulders, collar line, chest, girth, and breeching area for heat, rubs, or broken skin. He also **wipes the sweat and grit off the harness leather** so tomorrow's work doesn't rub them raw.
- **Legs & Hooves:** He runs his hands down each leg, feeling for heat, swelling, or a loose shoe. He picks each hoof from heel to toe, **checking the frog and clefts for thrush**: looking for a foul smell, black discharge, softness, or tenderness.

### 4. Feeding & Monitoring

- **The Gut:** He starts them with a **small hay ration (2-3 lb)** as a starter to keep the gut moving, arranging for proper full forage for the evening once they are fully settled.
- **Feeding Safety:** He strictly **holds off on the oats** until the horses are cooled, settled, **no longer blowing hard**, and drinking properly to avoid gut upset. He crumbles a few **dried apples** as a reward for the team.
- **The Final Watch:** Before heading to the inn, he does a quick sweep of the stall for dry bedding and good ventilation, and he checks their stance and behavior for any early signs of discomfort or colic.

> **The Result:** Only once the team is watered, checked, fed, and standing easy does Alphie dust off his hands, take up the Roane samples, and head for Northfurrow's End. He's ready for a drink himself, but the horses come first.

## Final assessment

This is **accurate, practical, and very believable** for Alphie. It should not be described as universally 100% correct for every horse in every condition, because horse care depends on heat, workload, age, condition, temperament, feed history, and how hard the team has travelled. But for this scene, it is husbandry-solid and table-safe.

The only tiny setting-sensitive wording adjustment was changing modern-sounding electrolyte certainty to:

> "if they've sweated particularly hard, he asks whether proper electrolytes are available to be mixed into feed or offered separately"

That keeps it realistic in a fantasy/medieval-ish stable where they may have salt, mineral mix, salted mash, or an equivalent rather than modern packaged electrolytes.

## Key fact-check conclusions

### Water after work

- Do **not** use the old myth that a hot horse must be denied water.
- Clean water should be offered promptly after work.
- The safer practical handling is supervised drinking in steady pauses while the horse cools, especially if the horse is winded or still blowing hard.
- Plain water must remain available and should not be replaced by salt or electrolytes.

### Cooling down

- Do not leave horses standing hot in harness.
- Unhook, loosen, and walk them until breathing settles and hard blowing eases.
- Avoid over-absolute language like "fully cooled" if the scene only requires them to be settled enough for safe feeding and stabling.

### Feeding

- Hay/forage first is sensible.
- A 2-3 lb hay ration is a starter, not the full evening feed.
- Oats/grain should be delayed until the horses are cooled, settled, no longer blowing hard, and drinking properly.
- The concern is better described as gut upset or colic risk, not cattle-style bloat.

### Hooves and thrush

- Hoof picking from heel to toe is fine scene wording.
- Thrush signs are best phrased as foul smell, black discharge, softness, tenderness, especially around the frog and clefts.
- Avoid awkward wording like "smelling for that specific black-ooze discharge".

### Harness and body checks

- Check shoulders, collar line, chest, girth/belly, breeching area, and any place leather has rubbed.
- Wipe sweat and grit from harness leather so dried salt and dirt do not cause sores tomorrow.
- Run hands down the legs for heat, swelling, cuts, tenderness, loose shoes, missing nails, stones, or packed mud.

### Wagon and cargo

- For a team/wagon setup, include singletrees and a doubletree/evener, plus pole, traces, brake, wheels, spokes, lynchpins, hubs, and axle play.
- Rocking wheels/axles gently to feel for play is better than "kicking the wheels".
- Tallow or heavy axle grease is believable for a medieval/fantasy wagon.
- Two 20L casks contain about 40 kg / 88 lb of liquid before wood/cask weight. Calling the total roughly a hundredweight is believable.
- The casks should stay low, wedged, and lashed so they cannot shift.

## Corrections made during the conversation

1. **Original cask weight corrected:** two 20L casks are not about 200 lb combined unless the casks are far larger/heavier. Roughly 100-120 lb including wood and liquid is more believable.
2. **Watering corrected:** replaced "2-4 quarts / 20-30% of needs" with immediate clean water, supervised drinking, and continuing access once calm.
3. **Bloat wording corrected:** replaced "bloat" with gut upset / colic risk.
4. **Hay wording corrected:** made clear 2-3 lb is a starter ration, not the full feed.
5. **Thrush wording corrected:** changed to foul smell, black discharge, softness, or tenderness around frog/clefts.
6. **Gear list improved:** added doubletree/evener for a three-horse wagon team.
7. **Electrolyte wording softened:** changed from assuming modern electrolytes are available to asking whether they are available, with salt/plain water always maintained.
