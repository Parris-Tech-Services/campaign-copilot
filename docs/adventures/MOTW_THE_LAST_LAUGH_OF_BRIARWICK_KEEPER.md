# THE LAST LAUGH OF BRIARWICK
### An original dark-fantasy horror-comedy mystery for Monster of the Week
**Keeper edition — complete spoilers.** Three hunters (Dylan, Gilbert, Josh), one first-time Keeper. **17:00–22:00 maximum; complete experience achievable by 20:00 (3 hours).**  
**Unofficial original material.** Inspired by *Amnesty*'s tonal approach, not a reproduction of its world, plot or characters.

> **Keeper in one sentence:** A village's annual comedy festival reawakens a creature made from an old cover-up: it steals people's shadows and voices to build its perfect audience.

## QUICK-START CARD (read before anything else)

- **Location:** Briarwick, a timber-framed town in the haunted north woods of a fantasy world. Festival: **The Mirthwake**, held once a year.
- **Immediate hook:** The town crier laughs uncontrollably, cries for help, and his shadow bows independently. A music-box melody drifts from the abandoned theatre.
- **Monster:** **The Morrowmummer**, a spectral masked stage-director who wants Briarwick's citizens as silent puppets in an eternal performance.
- **Cause:** A newly restored stage organ plays a forbidden melody. The town's founding tale hides the real death of **Mara Vell**, an entertainer who saved townspeople from a theatre fire after the reeve locked its doors.
- **Weakness — two actions:** **Silence the stage organ** (jam, destroy, sabotage, disable or magically quiet it); **tell Mara's true story aloud to three or more living witnesses** (hunters count). The monster becomes permanently vulnerable as the story is heard. It can then be banished, reasoned with, or defeated. The order of these two actions doesn't matter.
- **Don't gate either truth or method behind a single roll:** Archive inscription, gravekeeper testimony, old playbill, villain's diary, and the monster's speech all support the story. The organ's black threads, sound, festival schedule and clockmaker's drawings all identify the source.
- **Countdown:** Six stages; the monster ultimately traps the town as an eternal puppet audience.
- **Finale:** The abandoned theatre and its clockwork organ. **At 19:10 if on a 3-hour session; by 21:15 if using the full window.**
- **One possible opening line:** “The village has elected a goose as honorary magistrate. That is the least strange thing you see today.”
- **Keep play moving:** State a disturbing image; ask “What do you do?”; trigger the actual official hunter move when appropriate; use honest Keeper moves in response.
- **Safety:** Horror includes coercion, smoke/fire history, vanished voices and peril. Agree on lines/veils and allow anyone to skip or soften content. No sexual violence, gore or harm to children is required.

## 1. BEFORE PLAY — 17:00 TO 17:25

**17:00–17:08.** Welcome players, describe tone: “An odd little town. Folk-horror mystery. Genuine peril. Occasional very stupid jokes. We can pause or skip content without explaining.” Ask for boundaries and comfort level.

**17:08–17:25.** Select three **distinct** official Hunter playbooks, for example **The Mundane** (resourceful ordinary person), **The Spell-Slinger** (dangerous magic), **The Professional** (trained hunter). Alternatively choose any official playbooks; don't impose roles. Use the [official free playbooks and reference sheets](https://evilhat.com/product/monster-of-the-week/). If creating characters will take longer, bring already chosen playbooks to the table or trim the optional scenes. Modern-looking equipment can be fictionally reskinned to fantasy equivalents by agreement, while retaining mechanical rules.

Ask **each** hunter:
1. Why did you come to Briarwick's festival?
2. Which other hunter do you trust, and what did they do to earn it?
3. What's your first reaction when the supernatural becomes undeniable?

Record their answers and reuse one later. No prewritten backstory is mandatory.

**Quick reminder:** Players roll **2d6 + the relevant rating** when a move triggers; consult each actual move's 10+/7–9/miss text. The Keeper doesn't roll for NPC attacks. The Keeper's job is to respond to actions, portray threats truthfully, and give the three hunters equal spotlight.

## 2. OPENING — 17:25 TO 17:45

### Read aloud

> The road into Briarwick smells of wet leaves, roasting chestnuts and something unmistakably burnt.
>
> Paper lanterns sway between crooked houses. Every window bears the same painted symbol: a broad red smile.
>
> A placard at the gate reads: **“THE MIRTHWAKE. LAUGH OR PAY A SMALL MUNICIPAL FINE.”**
>
> In the market square, a goose in a tiny judicial wig sits upon a velvet cushion. An official solemnly announces that the goose is **Magistrate Honk**, appointed by popular demand after a disputed election.
>
> The town crier raises a scroll and laughs. And laughs. And laughs.
>
> His face is terrified.
>
> “Ha—ha—HELP ME—ha—”
>
> The shadow at his feet stands up, bows to an invisible crowd, and begins walking toward the boarded-up theatre.
>
> From that theatre comes a slow, tinkling tune.
>
> **What do you do?**

**What is immediately true:** Fennick the crier is being pulled by an invisible black thread stretching toward the theatre. His shadow can be held for a moment; cutting the thread or blocking the tune lets him catch his breath. He knows **Guildmaster Orren Pike reopened the theatre and hired a clockmaker to repair an old stage organ**. He does not know why the tune matters.

**Possible moves:** Save him with protection, clever gear, magic, distraction or physical intervention. Do not require a roll to notice the shadow/thread. If the hunters act decisively, let them rescue Fennick; danger shifts elsewhere.

**If everyone freezes:** The shadow turns and points at one of them; a masked figure briefly appears in a second-storey window. Cut to whichever hunter reacted.

## 3. THE TRUTH (KEEPER ONLY)

Seventy years ago, Briarwick's theatre caught fire during a public performance. Panicked reeve **Torvin Pike** ordered the doors barred to prevent a crush in the street. He never ordered them opened again, even when the smoke thickened. **Mara Vell**, an entertainer performing that night, broke a side door open and saved twelve people but died trying to rescue more. Torvin's family concealed the order and rewrote her story as a heroic sacrifice to “keep spirits high.” The Mirthwake festival now celebrates Torvin's supposed leadership with forced jokes and an old absurd song; few remember Mara's courage.

Collective denial and the cursed unfinished final performance gave life to **The Morrowmummer** — **not** Mara's ghost. It lives inside the laugh-painted mask on the antique stage organ. It steals the shadows and eventually the voices of those who hear the tune; it forces the town into the cheerful, obedient audience that the history books pretend existed. **It is not secretly a good creature.** It has learned to enjoy controlling people. Mara's story can weaken and expose it, not excuse its actions.

Two evenings ago, **Orren Pike** restored the organ for the festival and inadvertently reactivated the creature. Orren discovered an old order bearing his ancestor's signature, and hid it. He is responsible for today's denial, but can become an ally.

**Countdown ending if nobody intervenes:** Midnight creates a theatre larger than the town. Every person's shadow is sewn to the stage; their bodies sit unmoving in the audience. The Morrowmummer demands endless applause.

**Defeat/banish conditions (important):** Silence the organ **and** publicly acknowledge Mara's true rescue to at least three living witnesses. Each removes one anchor. Once both have happened, the monster's apparent invulnerability ceases: the hunters may banish it through a heartfelt address, negotiate a binding ending in good faith, or defeat its **9-harm / 1-armour** body in battle. A lone brawler cannot permanently destroy it while both anchors remain. Truthful witness does not require Orren's confession; *any* person with evidence can speak. Hunters count as listeners, so they cannot get stuck because townspeople have vanished.

## 4. THE PEOPLE OF BRIARWICK

### Etta Bram — innkeeper, helper
Owns **The Bent Spoon**. Big heart; cooking is so bad it has acquired theological detractors. **Wants:** to keep guests alive and stop the cursed tune. **Knows:** her grandmother said Mara Vell carried people through a side exit; has a charred menu on which Mara scribbled a note about the fire. **Voice:** “Look, the stew is safe. The *table* is haunted, but the stew is fine.”

### Fennick Bogle — town crier, witness
Wears a ceremonial hat taller than himself. **Wants:** never to be forced to laugh again. **Knows:** Orren restarted the restored theatre organ; melody comes from a hidden vent under the stage. **Voice:** “I must lodge an official complaint. I am, unfortunately, also the complaints department.”

### Sable Fen — gravekeeper, ally
Quiet, solemn and practical. Talks to plants; distrusts the festival. **Wants:** Mara's grave inscription restored and the innocent saved. **Knows:** Mara broke the side door; Torvin ordered the main doors barred. Can show the defaced epitaph and identify an old stage drawing. **Voice:** “The dead keep secrets only because the living stop asking.”

### Orren Pike — festival guildmaster, obstructive but redeemable
Expensive coat; ribbon everywhere; visibly sleep-deprived. **Wants:** the Mirthwake to succeed without family disgrace. **Knows:** has his ancestor's signed order and the clockmaker's diagram in a locked desk backstage. He reactivated the organ. Can surrender the documents when confronted; may confess if shown how many people are in danger. **Voice:** “This is an event-management concern. We do not, technically, have a department for haunted applause.”

### Magistrate Honk — goose, comic omen
A real goose wearing a tiny judicial wig, chosen by popular vote. **Wants:** stale bread and order. **Knows:** no words, but has a black puppet thread wrapped round one webbed foot; repeatedly pecks the route to the theatre. **Voice:** “HONK.” Do not make the goose secretly omniscient. Let it cause one useful comic interruption; that's enough.

### Mara Vell — memory / spectral trace
Mara herself is gone. The hunters might see a brief image of her holding a side door open in the archive's fire-scorched mirror. This isn't an NPC they must persuade, only a clue showing what happened.

## 5. KEY LOCATIONS — OPEN ORDER

The hunters can freely travel, split up or go directly to the theatre. Each location holds **at least one actionable clue**, and information can travel through witnesses/monstrous effects when the group misses a site.

### A. Market Square — hub
**Sensory image:** muddy bunting, cider stands, wilted roses, people laughing slightly out of sync.
- **Clue:** every affected shadow has a black thread extending invisibly toward the boarded-up theatre; under a lantern it becomes visible.
- **Clue:** Fennick's schedule says the organ's “Last Laugh” performance begins as the festival reaches its midnight climax.
- **Danger:** a laughing baker briefly loses their voice; the bread rises into an ominous standing ovation.
- **Comic beat:** Magistrate Honk steals Orren's official seal; the town treats this as lawful.

### B. The Bent Spoon Inn — hub
**Sensory image:** fragrant smoke, dented copper pans, chalkboard warning “NO NECROMANCY IN THE PANTRY — AGAIN”.
- **Clue:** Etta's charred old menu says “Mara, back door by the scenery; the front is locked.”
- **Clue:** a guest heard Orren say the “truth is not good for ticket sales.”
- **Help:** Etta knows a servant entrance to the theatre and can shelter people with stolen shadows.
- **Risk:** the song comes out of an empty ale barrel and begins tugging the cook's shadow.

### C. Graveyard / Chapel Archive — revelation
**Sensory image:** white moss, shallow carved names, soot caught between stone letters.
- **Clue:** Mara's grave originally read “She opened the door when others held it shut”; “opened” has been chiselled out.
- **Clue:** an old parish record lists twelve survivors and the signatures of Mara's witnesses.
- **Vision:** touching the burnt copper stage-key gives a brief impression of a **barred door**, smoke, and Mara opening the side exit.
- **Help:** Sable can tell Mara's story to three witnesses if she reaches the theatre; **not required**.
- **Threat:** a paper silhouette crawls out of the archive and attacks the book, not immediately a hunter.

### D. Old Theatre — finale / den
**Sensory image:** a collapsed proscenium, black velvet, dozens of empty chairs facing a brightly painted organ.
- **Clue:** the stage organ plays by itself; all puppet threads converge at its pipe chest.
- **Clue:** a black-and-white diagram identifies a pressure valve, bellows and hand-crank. The organ may be jammed, pulled apart, flooded, burned, dispelled or disabled by any plausible method. **Never require a single magic key.**
- **Clue:** Orren's desk holds Torvin Pike's signed locking order, a perfect witness to the truth.
- **Danger:** chairs turn to face individual hunters; audience silhouettes clap with bone-dry hands.
- **Escape:** stage trapdoor leads into a cramped storage tunnel and out to the inn's yard.

### E. Costume Cellar — OPTIONAL side scene (add only if session is ahead of schedule)
**Sensory image:** mannequins with names on tags, a cracked mirror showing characters still laughing after their faces stop.
- Rescue a missing stagehand, **Milo Reed**, alive but voiceless; he can identify how the organ is fed from below.
- A thread-bound mannequin (one minion) guards an old prop blade and Mara's handwritten testimony.
- Optional choice: destroy incriminating documents for Orren or carry them to the square.
- **Remove this entire scene in the three-hour version.**

### Simple relationship map
`Square -> Inn -> Archive -> Theatre -> Costume Cellar (optional)`. All routes also lead to the Theatre. The whole village is a fifteen-minute walk end to end. The Keeper should not require a cartographic puzzle.

## 6. CLUE REDUNDANCY — NEVER BOTTLENECK THE FINALE

| Required discovery | First path | Second path | Third path |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The theatre organ drives the curse** | Fennick saw Orren restore it | black threads converge there | mechanism vibrates each time a shadow moves |
| **Mara Vell died saving villagers** | Etta's menu | graveyard inscription and parish record | Orren's locked ancestral order |
| **The town needs a public truthful witness** | Sable: “the truth must be heard” | Mara's stage script insists “the audience must know” | the monster visibly recoils from a sincere account of the fire |
| **There is a safe way to stop the organ** | diagram of bellows and pipes | musician recognises the crank | hunter improvises reasonable sabotage / magic |

**On a miss:** reveal the clue **plus** a cost: lost time, a witness seized, dangerous proximity to the creature, the show advancing, or harm justified by the fiction. Never respond “you find nothing” where that would end the mystery.

**Do not force a confession from players about real life.** The truth is about fictional Mara, not the hunters' actual experiences.

## 7. MONSTER AND MINIONS — KEEPER STATS

### The Morrowmummer
**Threat type:** Collector — to steal shadows and voices and make an eternal audience.  
**Look:** A too-tall masked theatre director with a painted smile, nine-fingered gloves, and a coat sewn from torn playbills. A row of teeth clicks behind the mask, but nobody has ever seen a mouth.  
**Powers:** Draws shadows along invisible strings; moves through drapes and painted scenery; twists applause into a thunderous impact; can speak with a stolen voice.  
**Harm capacity:** **9**; **armour:** **1**; **attacks:** long thread lash **2-harm, close**; shears **3-harm, hand, messy**; wave of splintering applause **2-harm, area, close** (use sparingly).  
**Weakness:** Both anchors — **silent organ + true public acknowledgement of Mara's rescue before at least 3 living witnesses** — must be broken to allow permanent banishment or destruction. Prior damage can force retreat but not permanently end the threat.  
**Fair Keeper moves:** target an NPC, split a party, seize a clue, drag a shadow toward the organ, impose a hard choice, escape through stage drapes, interrupt a speech with terrifying applause.

**Possible bargain:** It may offer to return stolen shadows if **someone** performs for it forever. Make it a genuine moral choice rather than requiring sacrifice; hunters can negotiate a different way (a memory kept, a stage closed, the true tale performed each year) and the monster's acceptance should depend on believable leverage. **If a hunter offers themself, discuss consent and consequences at the table; avoid surprise forced character retirement.**

### Thread-Dolls
**Minion type:** Guardians — to guard the organ and carry stolen shadows.  
**Look:** human-shaped clothing stuffed with sawdust and trailing black thread.  
**Harm:** **2-harm, hand** (scissor fingers). **Harm capacity:** **5**. **Armour:** **0**. **Weakness:** cutting their heart-thread drops them instantly; fire also destroys their bodies.  
**Use:** one doll in a 3-hour session; a second only if the team is comfortably in control or running the longer version.

### Custom atmospheric effects
- **Stolen shadow:** Victim becomes hoarse, then voiceless; their shadow moves towards the theatre while their body remains alive. Cutting a thread delays the creature but the full rescue requires defeating it.
- **The laugh-track:** When characters intentionally keep their composure amid compulsive supernatural laughter, *Act Under Pressure* may trigger. Do not roll simply for hearing music. Let clever earplugs, counter-music or magic work if plausible.
- **Truth burns:** After an honest account of Mara's deed reaches witnesses, the monster's painted grin cracks. The fiction clearly signals the weakness worked.

## 8. COUNTDOWN — WHAT HAPPENS IF NO ONE STOPS IT

1. **Day:** Fennick's shadow stands and bows. The stage organ resumes.
2. **Shadows:** Another villager laughs helplessly and loses their voice.
3. **Sunset:** Two shadows escape into the old theatre; a Thread-Doll emerges in the market.
4. **Dusk:** The festival's crowd begins a synchronised involuntary performance.
5. **Nightfall:** Orren loses his shadow; the organ's music reaches every house.
6. **Midnight:** The theatre grows to surround the entire village. Bodies freeze into seats while shadows become eternal puppets.

**Advance the fictional countdown for time, off-screen action, or a meaningful consequence, not after every failed roll.** If the group goes straight to the theatre, skip intermediate events and let them avert the later stages.

## 9. PACING — 3-HOUR AND 5-HOUR TRACKS

### Essential path: EXACTLY 17:00–20:00 (three-hour target)
- **17:00–17:25** safety + rapid characters + bonds
- **17:25–17:45** market-square cold open; shadow rescue
- **17:45–18:35** visit two sites, reveal organ and fire
- **18:35–19:10** confront Orren / obtain enough proof, commit to a plan
- **19:10–19:50** old theatre climax and rescue
- **19:50–20:00** consequences, favourite image, advancement prompts

### Optional expansion to 21:00 or 22:00
- **+20–35 minutes:** detailed NPC conversations, rival stagehand, fuller social stakes at the inn.
- **+25–40 minutes:** Costume Cellar with Milo and one Thread-Doll.
- **+20–35 minutes:** side crisis in which the cursed crowd marches into the theatre; save bystanders.
- **+15–25 minutes:** extended bargaining and epilogue with the town facing its history.
- **Maximum rule:** By **21:15**, begin the theatre confrontation regardless of missed clues. Speak missing facts through surviving witnesses or evidence already at the scene. **Aim to conclude by 21:50 and debrief by 22:00.** If starting later than 17:00, shorten optional scenes first.

### Clock-driven Keeper rescue prompts
**18:30:** “As the melody repeats, every affected shadow points at the theatre.”  
**19:00:** “Sable arrives with the defaced inscription; the story of Mara can now be told.”  
**20:30 (long mode):** “A surviving witness delivers Orren's order; the whole village hears the organ.”  
**21:15 hard converge:** “The Morrowmummer calls the three hunters to the theatre. Both the organ and its lie are openly before them.”

These are fallback prompts **only if the table stalls**. Do not override a credible earlier success.

## 10. FINALE — 19:10 TO 19:50, OR 21:15 TO 21:50

### Read aloud on entering the theatre

> The doors shut behind you with a gentle click, as though somebody has remembered their manners.
>
> A hundred empty chairs face the stage. Then the chairs begin to applaud.
>
> The organ plays three impossible notes. Onstage stands a figure in a painted porcelain mask, conducting thin black threads with fingers far too long.
>
> The figure bows.
>
> “AT LAST,” says a chorus of stolen voices. “AN AUDIENCE THAT CAME VOLUNTARILY.”
>
> Its mask turns from one of you to the next.
>
> “EVERYONE SMILE.”
>
> What do you do?

### Support agency and give everybody a job
- **Hunter A:** bypass a doll and interrupt the mechanism.
- **Hunter B:** assemble living listeners / deliver the real story.
- **Hunter C:** protect captives and face the Morrowmummer.
These are **opportunities**, not locked character assignments. Follow the group's own plan. The theatre has several available witnesses: Orren, Sable, Etta, Fennick, rescued Milo or the three hunters themselves.

### Three legitimate endings
1. **Release:** tell Mara's story and silence the organ; call the truth what it is. The Morrowmummer's threads unravel and shadows return. No combat is required if the fiction supports its dissolution.
2. **Defeat:** once its weakness is in effect, fight it using its 9-harm/1-armour profile. A dying doll may make a final bid to keep the organ playing.
3. **A costly compromise:** negotiations save the village in exchange for an ongoing obligation. Make consequences clear, give hunters an informed choice, and complete the *current* mystery even if a sequel hook remains.

### If they lose
Briarwick becomes a puppet theatre as promised. **This is a dramatic failure, not an invitation to extend the session beyond 22:00.** Describe who escapes, what the hunters saved, and one clue for a future rescue if they ever want another mystery.

### Epilogue questions (5–10 minutes)
- Which bystander does each hunter check on?
- Does the village tell the truth about Mara?
- What new personal bond has formed?
- If playing again: a single discarded black thread tightens around a distant signpost. **Do not invalidate tonight's victory.**

Use the official end-of-session experience prompts (see free Keeper sheet).

## 11. IMPROVISATION AND FIRST-TIME KEEPER CHEAT SHEET

**Scene loop:** Vivid detail -> dangerous or strange thing -> ask “What do you do?” -> fictionally appropriate hunter move -> narrate specific consequence -> spotlight somebody else.

**When a hunter fails:** Make a move that matches the fiction: somebody gets caught, useful gear drops, an NPC panics, the monster steals time, or the story advances. **Do not narrate a random failure just to punish rolling.**

**When a hunter asks “Can I...?”** Often say yes if the fictional method is plausible; if genuinely risky, identify the appropriate official move and clear stakes.

**When they ignore the clue:** Move it. Fennick can run in; Orren can blurt a secret; shadow threads can plainly point; the ghost can show a vision. Don't fake a clue that contradicts the underlying mystery.

**When they kill Orren:** The original order and other independent clues remain; the town can tell the truth without his cooperation.

**When they smash the organ early:** Great! One anchor gone. The creature weakens visibly but still requires the public story to end permanently.

**When they tell the story early:** Great! Its mask cracks; the organ still draws shadow threads until it is silenced.

**When all hunters rush a fight:** Show why the creature reforms, then offer obvious investigative evidence of its anchors. Don't make an unwinnable combat last an hour.

**When running out of time:** Reveal the truth through an honest NPC or the original order, bring everyone to the theatre, and finish. Never ask players to re-roll an already resolved clue.

**When running 3 hours:** Skip the Cellar, run only one doll, move the essential evidence into the first two investigative scenes, and begin the finale by 19:10.

**When running 5 hours:** Include the Cellar, one social crisis and a longer debate around Orren, but preserve the finale cutoff by 21:15.

## 12. PLAYER-SAFE OPENING HANDOUT (CUT OUT OR SHARE SEPARATELY)

**THE LAST LAUGH OF BRIARWICK**  
*A strange village, a festival of bad jokes, and the sense that somebody else is laughing with your voice.*

You have arrived in Briarwick for the annual Mirthwake celebration. The town advertises lanterns, music, feasting and official municipal humour. The roads through the forest are treacherous, strangers are uncommon, and the old theatre has been closed for as long as anyone can remember.

At the village gate a sign warns: **“LAUGH OR PAY A SMALL MUNICIPAL FINE.”**

Bring a hunter with a reason to be here. Answer: Why Briarwick? Which other hunter do you trust? What is the strangest thing you have ever encountered?

**Tone:** folk horror + dark fantasy + occasional absurd comedy. **Time:** 17:00–20:00 minimum session, extendable until 22:00. **Players:** Dylan, Gilbert and Josh. **Keeper:** host.

*Do not read the Keeper material if you want to preserve the mystery.*
