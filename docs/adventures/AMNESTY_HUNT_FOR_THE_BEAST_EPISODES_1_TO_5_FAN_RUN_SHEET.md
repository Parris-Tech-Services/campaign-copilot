# The Adventure Zone: Amnesty — Hunt for the Beast
## FLEXIBLE first-time Keeper guide | 3 hunters | 3–5 hours | 17:00–22:00

**VERSION 2 — 2026-10-25. This replaces the earlier separate-scene/character-assignment run sheet.**

> **ONE RULE:** Run the **situation**, not the podcast script. Dylan, Gilbert and Josh each create **any** legal Monster of the Week hunter. They **begin together**, decide what to investigate, can move anywhere and invent solutions. The episode 1–5 events are possible developments, not requirements. The Beast still has its original nature, context and fire weakness. Other choices produce their own genuine endings.

**System:** Monster of the Week, not D&D. **Setting:** Contemporary Kepler, West Virginia, Monongahela National Forest. **Source basis:** original *Amnesty* episodes 1–5; primary show pages linked at end. **Unofficial fan session aid:** This is not Griffin McElroy's published prep, exact transcript or official stats. Marked [ADAPTED] means original bridging material for three new players, and [PODCAST] means a documented story element. **Full spoilers for Keeper only.**

## 1. THE KEEPER'S SECRET (read first)

- [PODCAST] The **Beast** is an enormous, malformed patchwork animal Abomination in the forest near Kepler, made up of animal parts and a black ooze/slime inner body. It infects or absorbs other animals; its eyes glow unnaturally. It is **vulnerable to fire**. In the original story the hunters lure it to **Crooked Bend Cave**, burn away its outer mass, reveal an awful lizard-like core, then overcome it. The original ending is **only one possible outcome** here.
- [PODCAST] **Mama** and **Barclay** operate with the **Pine Guard** at **Amnesty Lodge** and know about incursions called Abominations. **Barclay** is Bigfoot, but is **friendly**, as are other supernatural lodge inhabitants called Sylphs. The stone gateway reaches **Sylvain**. Do **not** make the lodge's allies secretly enemies for a shock twist; preserve the central setting.
- [PODCAST] The first recorded party comprised ranger **Duck Newton**, fire magician **Aubrey Little**, and cryptid enthusiast **Ned Chicane**. Their personal material (Minerva, Beacon, the Cryptonomica, a Wookiee outfit) is **optional colour**, not a prerequisite and **not assigned to particular players**.
- [ADAPTED] **Threat objective:** animal instinct to feed, grow and escape its initial territory; it is **not** a mastermind with dialogue or a negotiable political agenda. It grows more dangerous the longer unchecked.
- [ADAPTED] **Mystery questions to answer:** What is attacking animals and people? How is it linked to the woods/gateway? What can hurt/stop it? Where and when can the hunters confront it on favourable terms?
- **Essential win condition:** permanently stop the Beast by applying/using its fire weakness while overcoming or otherwise containing its true body. There is no required hero, sword, cave, Wookiee outfit, gasoline ring, encounter order, or particular final blow. If hunters invent a plausible permanent containment strategy, allow that ending and its consequences.

## 2. THREE NEW HUNTERS, ONE SHARED OPENING

### Character creation — 17:00–17:25 (15 minutes with prechosen sheets)
Let Dylan, Gilbert and Josh select **ANY THREE** official MotW playbooks. The Spooky, Mundane, Expert, Wronged, Professional, Searcher, Flake, Chosen, Crooked, Spell-Slinger etc. all work. If all choose similar hunters, let it work: change how clues appear, not what the answer is. Use official free playbooks and reference sheets: https://evilhat.com/product/monster-of-the-week/ .

Ask each, briefly:
1. **What has brought you to Kepler?** Examples: holiday, strange case, vanished relative, local job, cryptid convention, visiting a friend.
2. **Why are you all together when the trouble starts?** They are already acquainted, were hired together, or each answered the same call. Pick one group explanation together — **no separate solo origin scenes**.
3. **What personal thing would make you care whether Kepler survives?** A person, curiosity, vow, sense of justice, financial stake, etc.

**[ADAPTED, shared opening — read aloud]**
> It is late afternoon in Kepler, West Virginia. Outside, mountains rise above a little town that is trying very hard to look normal. The three of you are together when a mud-splattered camper staggers in asking for help. She is shaking. “Something attacked our campsite. It looked like a bear, but it wasn't right. Pete is missing.” Black slime drips from the sleeve of her jacket. An engine is still running outside. She keeps looking over her shoulder, as if whatever chased her might already be here. **What do you do?**

This is a **new joining scene**, not an exact recording of Episode 1. The frightened camper is **Pidgeon** and Pete is her missing companion [PODCAST]. Pick a convenient shared gathering place: **outside the Cryptonomica**, **the ranger station**, **a diner**, **the lodge car park**, or **wherever the hunters say they are**. The same event comes to them.

**If they refuse to help:** That's a real choice. Pidgeon contacts the ranger station; another animal is attacked; a local asks for urgent help. The threat progresses by countdown. If they choose to leave town, reveal a road-blocking animal incident and let them decide to intervene or escape. Never teleport them back into the plot without cause.

## 3. SITUATION BOARD — AN OPEN NETWORK, NOT FIVE EPISODE SCENES

Start with Pidgeon's testimony. Ask “Where do you go first?” Everything below is reachable, and any sequence works.

| Node | What they encounter | Information available | What can change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pidgeon's campsite** | ripped tents, Pete missing, enormous mixed tracks, black slime, scorched ground | animal-fused shape; evidence **fire frightens/hurts it** | rescue Pete, follow tracks, call police, set bait, abandon it |
| **Kepler town** | ranger office, medical aid, Cryptonomica, locals, vehicles, supplies | patterns of missing wildlife, Lodge connection, material resources | win townsfolk, raise alarm, incur public panic, arrange a trap |
| **Amnesty Lodge** | Mama, Barclay and eccentric but friendly residents | **Abominations** and **Pine Guard**; gateway; monster lives near woods; field help | recruit allies, antagonise or mistrust them, steal resources |
| **Forest trails / infected wildlife** | odd silence, animal fusion, black ooze, frightened creatures | where Beast hunts; where it might go; **fire weakness** shown directly | rescue someone, get chased, take a sample, discover feeding pattern |
| **Stone arch / gateway** | strange light, protective secrets, access to **Sylvain** | supernatural origin of incursion; danger is not an ordinary animal | cross, bargain for help, guard/close gate, avoid it |
| **Crooked Bend Cave** | secluded cave with choke points, potential ambush site | a defensible location, **not** a quest objective | trap it here, choose another site, lose access after collapse |
| **Any hunter-invented location** | fire station, hunting store, mine, bridge, sawmill, power lines etc. | transferable facts from the closest node | **Reward a credible plan**; don't require them to visit planned sites |

**Never say:** “You cannot go there, Episode 3 hasn't happened.”  
**Do say:** “Yes, here's what that place is like. What are you looking for?”

### Four fact tracks: award in any order

1. **Nature:** Beast is not a normal bear; looks assembled, black slime binds animal bodies. Sources: campsite evidence / ranger witness / direct sight / Mama's account.
2. **Where:** affected wildlife and giant tracks cluster by forest arch; it can travel into nearby roads. Sources: trail / Lodge map / captured slime / gateway watch.
3. **Weakness:** fire burns the patchwork and exposes core. Sources: Pidgeon's campfire / scorch marks / improvised flame / Mama / the Beast itself recoiling.
4. **Opportunity:** hunters can lure or intercept it through bait, scent, animal calls, observation or magic, preferably away from bystanders. Sources: infected animal behaviour / expert / improvised hunter method / Mama's map.

**Clues are not locks.** Important facts can be found in two or more places. A missed roll can deliver a real clue while adding danger, cost, time loss or another consequence consistent with the actual official move; do not change the printed move's prescribed outcome.

## 4. RUN THE NPCs BY MOTIVATION (NOT BY SCRIPT)

- **Pidgeon — worried witness:** wants Pete back; saw the animal horror. If rescued, grateful; if ignored, asks others for help. Does not need Duck to exist.
- **Pete — person in danger:** missing near the woods; alive or in peril depending on hunters' choices/countdown. Do not assume he must die to match a podcast.
- **Mama — Pine Guard leader:** wants civilians safe and Abominations stopped without exposing Sylphs. Offers help when trust is earned; will still protect the town if hunters are rude or avoid Lodge.
- **Barclay — friendly lodge cook/Bigfoot:** wants to protect his found family and avoid public exposure. May reveal his form to save somebody; **doesn't need Ned's fake documentary**.
- **Dani and Jake Coolice — lodge residents:** offer social warmth, odd humour, minor information; not all residents need scenes.
- **Duck, Aubrey, Ned — optional cameos ONLY:** If your players create other hunters, the three canonical leads do **not** need to appear. If used as NPCs, make them brief helpers, never protagonists who solve the case.
- **Minerva/Beacon — optional lore:** Only bring these in if a player deliberately takes a Chosen/destiny direction, or as flavour in a later campaign. Do not graft Duck's destiny onto an unwilling new character.
- **Vincent / Sylvain — optional if gate explored:** What lies beyond the gate can be disclosed in small portions; you need not conduct a full otherworld expedition for a 3-hour session.

### Natural silly moments that don't ruin the stakes
A Cryptonomica exhibit is obviously fake **beside genuine monster slime**; a lodge resident is disproportionately worried about kitchen rules during a supernatural emergency; a local deputy insists on normal wildlife incident forms for an impossible footprint. Offer these when action eases. Keep humour emergent and brief, not a compulsory joke scene.

## 5. CLOCK AND COUNTDOWN: EVENTS WHEN THE HUNTERS DON'T STOP IT

**These are [ADAPTED] MotW countdown consequences, not a transcript of Episodes 1–5.** Advance when fictional time elapses, a major opportunity is ignored, or hunters choose to delay. It is not a punishment for one bad roll.

| Stage | If no one acts |
|---|---|
| Day | Pidgeon escapes an attack; Pete is missing; animal tracks and black slime remain. |
| Shadows | corrupted forest wildlife move toward the Beast; a livestock owner reports an impossible creature. |
| Sunset | the Beast absorbs another animal and grows; trails leading to the gate become dangerous. |
| Dusk | a witness is attacked at a road or campsite; frightened locals consider fleeing. |
| Nightfall | the Beast crosses to the edge of Kepler, directly threatening people and supplies. |
| Midnight | the creature reaches a populated area or escapes the hunters' control; casualties become likely. |

**If they attack immediately:** Do not insist on later stages. **If they visit the gate first:** They may learn the otherworld secret before meeting Mama. **If they save Pete early:** He can point out scorched tracks or its feeding pattern. **If they isolate and trap the Beast on the first hour:** Let the early win stand.

## 6. KEEP THE MONSTER FAIR AND RESOLVABLE

**The Beast [PODCAST identity / ADAPTED stats]:** patchwork of animals around a black, lizard-like slime core; hunts and assimilates animals; responds to scent; hurt badly by fire. It does not deliver villain monologues.

**Optional quick reference stats — homebrew, NOT Griffin's original sheet:**
- Monster: **10 harm capacity**, **1 armour**; crushing claws **3-harm hand**, rush **2-harm close**.
- Minion infected animal: **3 harm capacity**, **0 armour**, bite/claw **1-harm hand**.
- **Special:** ordinary injuries can briefly break its momentum, but **fire is required to expose and permanently defeat its core**. Once exposed, allow any narratively credible means of finishing, containing or banishing it; magical flame is not required.

Stats are first-time-Keeper suggestions, not official episode mechanics. If your chosen playbooks are fragile, reduce harm and add non-harm threats instead.

### Endings players can create

A. **Original-type ending:** Track/lure into Crooked Bend Cave; burn fused shell; battle inner form; resolve the danger.  
B. **Different battlefield:** Trap it at a bridge, quarry, logging road or clearing, and use flame safely there. The cave is **optional**.  
C. **Scientific/occult solution:** Study slime, use salt circles/rituals/fireproof confinement or gate lore to contain it. Make it costly but potentially valid, especially with a specialist hunter.  
D. **Rescue and retreat:** Save Pete and civilians, but Beast escapes; Kepler remains endangered. Honest partial victory is possible.  
E. **Failure:** Its countdown reaches Midnight. Decide what the hunters save, who remains, and whether next session would be a rescue hunt.  
F. **Unexpected brilliant plan:** Say yes when it follows the fictional facts. Don't override successes to re-enact the podcast.

**Guardrail:** This creature's **fire vulnerability** is a defining fact. A no-fire permanent kill needs a strong fictionally established substitute; don't convert every random attack into an instant kill. Conversely don't demand a specific fuel ring, cave or talking sword.

## 7. WHAT IF THEY DO SOMETHING UNEXPECTED? LIVE BRANCH TABLE

| Player action | Your immediate answer | Next meaningful consequence |
|---|---|---|
| “We phone the police / rangers.” | The call is answered. They can mobilise people. | Authorities help or create a witness/safety problem; Beast remains active. |
| “We investigate the Lodge first.” | Let them travel, meet Mama and Barclay. | Trust, supernatural truth, or protective secrecy determines their help. |
| “We don't trust Mama.” | She doesn't suddenly become evil. Ask what evidence they need. | They can find identical facts at the camp/gate/tracks. |
| “We use a different class or have no magic.” | Fine. **No required fire magician.** | Flares, torches, controlled burns, fuel, fireworks or local resources can exploit fire weakness without gratuitous arson. |
| “We split up.” | Follow the fiction; alternate spotlight frequently. | Each sees different clues; isolated hunters face greater danger. |
| “We sneak through the gateway.” | Describe the other world and watchful locals. | They gain origin knowledge; time in Kepler advances. |
| “We ask friendly monsters for help.” | Barclay/Mama can offer equipment or a distraction, never solve it offscreen. | Aid creates reciprocal obligations or secrecy risk. |
| “We steal an armoured vehicle / plant explosives.” | Ask where/how they obtain it; follow move rules. | Realistic physical and social fallout. A credible plan can work. |
| “We run away.” | They escape if fiction and rolls allow. | Show the Beast's next consequence without forcing their return. |
| “We kill the Beast immediately.” | If vulnerability and fiction truly allow it, it dies. | Move straight to aftermath: do not invent fake respawn because Ep 5 hasn't happened. |
| “We want to play a Monstrous hunter.” | Accepted. Friendly Sylphs demonstrate monsters needn't all be villains. | Mama initially assesses their trustworthiness rather than refusing to cooperate. |
| “We think there's another explanation.” | Let them test it. Tell the truth based on evidence. | Don't alter the Beast's nature to validate every guess. |

**Generic rescue response for any surprise:** “Yes, that's possible. How do you do it?” -> establish risk -> see if an official move triggers -> give a meaningful effect -> ask “What do you do?”

## 8. TIMEBOX: ONE HUNT, NOT FIVE EPISODE RECREATIONS

**Start 17:00. Minimum target 3 hours (finish around 20:00). Maximum stop 22:00.** These are **pacing checkpoints**, never mandatory locations/scenes.

| Real-world clock | Keeper pacing objective — independent of scene order |
|---|---|
| **17:00–17:25** | Create any characters, decide why the three are together, agree safety boundaries. |
| **17:25–18:15** | Present the shared incident; they choose the first leads; at least two significant discoveries and one choice with risk. |
| **18:15–18:55** | Develop their chosen inquiry; let them establish the creature is unnatural, identify its likely territory and get clear fire evidence. |
| **18:55–19:10** | In the **3-hour version**, prompt a hunter-made plan or immediate chance to confront Beast. Do not require all locations visited. |
| **19:10–19:50** | **3-hour climax:** hunter-chosen battlefield/approach, reveal exposed core, resolve hunt. |
| **19:50–20:00** | End of short run: aftermath + XP prompts. |
| **20:00–21:15** | **Only if playing longer:** more chosen investigation/relationships, additional crisis, travel/gate scenes, richer setup; don't invent filler if they already won. |
| **21:15** | **Hard deadline for long-run confrontation:** move active danger into play naturally (e.g. a fresh sighting near hunters) and make key clues available. Do not teleport PCs or undo choices. |
| **21:15–21:50** | Long version final confrontation, whatever approach they chose. |
| **21:50–22:00** | Epilogue; game ends by 22:00. |

**If hunter creation runs long:** drop optional NPC colour before squeezing investigation or ending.  
**If an excellent plan ends at 19:20:** enjoy the aftermath and do not force another 40 minutes. “3 hours minimum” is a scheduling aim, not a mandate to nullify success; allow time for character epilogues and reflection.  
**If at 21:45 no climax:** cut directly to the decisive present choice, resolve consequences honestly, epilogue in 5 minutes.

## 9. KEEPER RULES: FOLLOW THE HUNTERS, NOT THE SCRIPT

Use the **official free references** for full move wording. Core: 2d6 + rating on triggered moves; 10+ strong, 7–9 mixed according to move, miss invites Keeper move / XP according to applicable rules. The Keeper doesn't roll attacks. Make the world real, be a fan of the hunters, and play to see what happens.

### Five concrete scene loops
1. Describe an observable danger and **one interactable thing**.
2. Ask **“What do you do?”**, not “Which written scene next?”
3. If a move triggers, let the hunter roll and use the **actual printed move result**, not universal improvised 7–9 rules.
4. Change the situation: action affects NPCs, evidence, locations, creature, countdown.
5. Give a different hunter spotlight, and offer 2–3 obvious avenues **without limiting other ideas**.

**Example:** They attempt to lure it into a motel car park instead of Crooked Bend Cave. Ask how they prevent townsfolk being hurt; follow their plan; show how the Beast responds to animal scent and flame. If it works, **use motel car park as the finale**. They didn't “miss Episode 5”; they played their own hunt.

### Evidence contingency prompts (ADAPTED original wording)
- “The creature's trail seems to be made of **several animals walking as one**.”
- “A charred patch of earth is the only place those tracks abruptly veer away.”
- “The old lodge feels safe, but everybody here is **carefully choosing their words**.”
- “The sick bobcat's eyes flash the **same unnatural red** as the thing near camp.”
- “The beast doesn't just charge - it circles **toward the smell** of frightened animals.”
- “The forest map shows a **narrow cave**, an open quarry and a river crossing. Which ground would you rather control?”

### If they need help
If they stall, ask what their hunters are hoping to learn, not what the plot needs. Have Pidgeon volunteer another relevant observation; Mama offer a map; an infected animal lead towards the den; the next count-down event happen **in view**. Don't withhold crucial evidence behind a single roll or NPC.
